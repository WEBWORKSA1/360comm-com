How to Port Your Business Phone Number Without Downtime

Step-by-step number porting checklist: LOA, CSR, timing, and the mistakes that cause rejections.

360360Comm Editorial Team · Updated Sep 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Porting (Local Number Portability) moves your existing number to a new provider. Done right, customers never notice. Done wrong, your main line goes dark. Follow this checklist.

Before you start

  • Do not cancel your old service — cancelling can release the number.
  • Request a Customer Service Record (CSR) from your current carrier; the name, address and account number must match exactly.
  • Get your account PIN / port-out passcode (mobile carriers require it).
  • List every number: main lines, fax, toll-free, DIDs.

The porting process

  1. Set up users and call flows on the new platform using temporary numbers.
  2. Submit a Letter of Authorization (LOA) and a recent bill copy to the new provider.
  3. Wait for the Firm Order Commitment (FOC) date — typically days to a few weeks.
  4. On port day, test inbound calls, SMS and voicemail immediately.
  5. Cancel the old service only after all numbers are confirmed working.

Top reasons ports get rejected

  • Name or address mismatch with the CSR
  • Wrong account number or missing PIN
  • Partial port of a hunt group without specifying the billing telephone number
  • Numbers under contract or with a pending order
Tip: Schedule the port mid-week in the morning so support teams are available if anything goes wrong.
