The Hidden Costs of VoIP: 11 Fees That Inflate Your Phone Bill

Why your VoIP invoice is higher than the advertised price — taxes, regulatory fees, add-ons, minimum seats — and how to avoid each.

360360Comm Editorial Team · Updated Sep 30, 2026 · 7 min read

A $20/user plan rarely costs $20. Industry pricing guides consistently show that taxes, regulatory fees and add-ons push real invoices 15–25% above the headline — sometimes much more once hardware and extra numbers are included. Here is every line item to budget for.

Taxes and government fees

  1. Federal Universal Service Fund (USF) — a percentage of the interstate portion of your bill, set quarterly.
  2. State and local taxes — sales and telecom taxes vary widely by address.
  3. E911 fees — per-line fees that fund emergency services.
  4. Regulatory recovery fees — a provider-set charge to recover compliance costs.

Plan and usage add-ons

  1. Monthly vs annual billing — month-to-month often costs 20–40% more.
  2. Minimum seats — some plans require 2–3 licenses minimum.
  3. Extra numbers and toll-free minutes — additional DIDs and toll-free usage are frequently billed separately.
  4. SMS registration (10DLC) — brand and campaign registration fees plus per-message carrier fees.
  5. Call recording storage and AI — transcription, summaries and analytics are often paid add-ons.

One-time costs

  1. Hardware — desk phones and headsets ($80–$300+ per phone for business-grade models).
  2. Porting and setup — some carriers charge to port or for professional onboarding.
